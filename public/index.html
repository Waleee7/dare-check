002 / How the grade works

20 checks · 100 points

Each check is worth points. A pass earns all of them, a warning half, a fail none. Checks that can't run don't count against you. 90 or more is an A, 80 a B, 65 a C, 50 a D. A site with no working HTTPS, or one on a malware blocklist, gets an F no matter what else it gets right.

003 / Straight answers

Before you worry
I got a bad grade. Have I been hacked?

Almost certainly not. A low grade means doors were left unlocked, not that anyone came in. The exception is a blocklist failure, which can mean something bad is already on the site, so that one goes to the top of your fix list.

Can this hurt my site, or get me in trouble?

No. DARE CHECK loads your homepage over https and http, tries the www version of your address, and reads public records. Your site sees the same requests a few ordinary visitors would make. Nothing gets logged in, guessed at or changed.

Do you keep my results?

No. Nothing is stored on our side beyond a two-minute cache in memory. Your browser remembers your recent checks on this device so it can show what changed, and you can clear them. To run a check, the address you type goes to public services: DNS, domain registries, WordPress.org, and Cloudflare and Quad9's security filters.

Why does a huge company sometimes get a B?

Because the grade is about these specific basics, not how good a company is. Big sites often skip a header on purpose, or send people to a different address first. For most small sites, though, every item here is worth fixing.

If I get an A, am I safe?

You've covered the basics that stop the most common attacks, which puts you ahead of most sites. It isn't a full security audit: it doesn't look past your homepage, test your logins or read your code. Keep your software updated and your passwords long.
